# Food Pantry Visit Planner

A small, printable planning aid for a food pantry, mobile distribution, or community meal visit. It helps someone confirm details that can turn a short trip into a wasted one. The companion index.html runs entirely in the browser and stores nothing on a server.

## What to check before leaving

1. **Find the right service.** A food bank is often a warehouse supplying partner pantries; it may not hand groceries to visitors. A pantry or distribution site is the place to ask about public pickup.
2. **Verify the exact service window.** Office hours, donation hours, and public food distribution hours can differ. For a mobile event, confirm todays date, temporary address, cancellation notices, and last arrival time.
3. **Ask about access rules.** Check whether the site serves your ZIP code, requires an appointment or registration, and whether a walk-in, vehicle, or referral is needed. Requirements vary by provider.
4. **Plan the return trip.** Consider what you can carry, transit time, refrigeration, and any accessibility needs. Ask the provider about available accommodations.
5. **Keep a backup option.** If the first location is closed or full, contact your local 211 for another nearby food resource.

## How to use the printable page

Open index.html in a browser. Choose the type of food help you need, tick the questions you have confirmed, and print or save the checklist. Do not enter names, benefit numbers, addresses, or other private case information. The page is a planning aid, not a live directory, benefit application, or guarantee that food is available.

## Find and confirm a provider

- [Feeding Americas food bank locator](https://www.feedingamerica.org/find-your-local-foodbank) identifies the regional network. Follow through to the local pantry or meal program and confirm its current details.
- [United Way 211](https://211.org/) can connect you with local food and other community services.
- [Food Stamps Offices food bank visit guide](https://foodstampsoffice.org/food-bank/) explains the difference between a food bank and pantry and gives a longer verification checklist. **Disclosure:** I contribute to FoodStampsOffice.org, an independent information site. It is not a government agency or food provider.

Provider schedules and requirements can change. The providers current notice or direct answer should decide whether you travel.
